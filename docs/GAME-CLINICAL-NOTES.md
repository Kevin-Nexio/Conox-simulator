# Clinical teaching revision, 3 November 2026

Roman's feedback is the editorial basis for this revision. The opening case is
fictional: early severe ARDS, intubation, propofol, controlled lung-protective
ventilation and preparation for prone positioning. The treating team has chosen
a temporary RASS −4 goal. There is no neuromuscular blockade during the clinical
assessments. This is not a universal ARDS sedation prescription.

The four stages introduce the treatment goal, continuous alpha/delta EEG during
deep sedation, gradually increasing discontinuity, and a separate reassessment
endpoint. The sixty-second transition is a teaching sequence, not a drug
response model, dosing experiment or patient recording. Suppression is not the
treatment goal in this story. The final endpoint does not claim a particular
dose, interval or clinical outcome.

## Evidence and boundaries

[The ATS ARDS guideline](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10870893/)
supports lung-protective ventilation and prone positioning in severe ARDS, with
individualised treatment decisions. It does not justify assigning RASS −4 to
every patient with ARDS. The specific target is an explicit fictional case fact.

[The international ICU pEEG consensus](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12028-022-01565-5)
supports interpreting raw EEG, spectral information and indices with clinical
context. The story distinguishes clinical depth from EEG suppression and keeps
drug effects, artefacts and specific neurological indications in view. A frontal
monitor does not provide a complete neurological diagnosis. The existing DSA
remains a teaching spectral model, not a newly validated EEG-analysis algorithm.

[The qCON–RASS observational study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9067479/)
does not establish an individual fixed conversion; it did not evaluate the
distinction between appropriate sedation and RASS −4/−5 oversedation. No
qCON 65 = RASS −2 rule is introduced. Indices are masked in the narrative;
visible indices in controls exercises retain their illustrative reference role.

[The AHRQ awakening/breathing protocol](https://www.ahrq.gov/hai/tools/mvp/modules/technical/sat-sbt-protocol.html)
places safety screening before an awakening trial. The mission describes a later
clinical phase after the reason for deep sedation has resolved. EEG contributes
observations; it does not determine trial eligibility, stopping rules or
extubation readiness. No drug doses are taught.

## Editorial choices

Blood pressure, heart rate, oxygenation and ventilator interaction are retained
as clinical context, not converted into sedation-depth scores. Cough, pressure
support triggering and asynchrony prompt assessment; they do not establish pain,
awareness or an automatic need for deeper sedation.

Trend review asks the learner to relate EEG changes to documented treatment
events. A dose–effect mismatch or plateau warrants review; the game does not
claim that Conox diagnoses tachyphylaxis or a ceiling effect. An abrupt change
after a bolus is a timing clue, not proof of causation. The advisory remains an
additional reminder rather than the central teaching objective.

The revised material has software and browser checks; it is not represented as
independently clinically validated or as a substitute for local protocols.
